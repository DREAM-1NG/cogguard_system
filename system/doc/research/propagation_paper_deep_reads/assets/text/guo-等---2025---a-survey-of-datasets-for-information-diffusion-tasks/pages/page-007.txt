5
Misinformation detection
For the content during the information diffusion process, the decen-
tralization of discourse has led to an increase in false or misleading
information (i.e., misinformation), which not only misguides the
public but also threatens cyberspace security, making misinfor-
mation detection essential [100, 101]. Misinformation primarily
includes rumors and fake news [102]. Rumors are unverified, un-
intentionally spread on social media, while fake news consists of
deliberately false articles spread by official accounts or websites [13].
Therefore, misinformation detection is divided into rumor and fake
news detection.
Current methods rely on a single information source [103] by
incorporating post content and contextual information. Specifically,
social context includes repost/comment content, user context (i.e.,
user information), and network context (i.e., user social network,
diffusion network, and user engagement set) [13, 44, 81, 88, 104].
Based on these, rumor detection determines the veracity label of
a post 𝑦𝑃∈{0, 1}, indicating a non-rumor (0) or a rumor (1). Fake
news detection classifies news as real (0) or fake (1) [80].
