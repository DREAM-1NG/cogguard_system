   3.1.2  Publication Time. Another important temporal feature is the publication time t0. As re-
ported in previous works [109, 137, 181, 186, 190], the popularity of a certain item is strongly
related to its publication time, e.g., items published at midnight are less likely to be viewed while
items published during daytime are generally more popular (though they have more competition)
[13, 62, 210, 219]. To mitigate the effect of user activity periodicity, several solutions have been
proposed. [155] designed 24 local models, each of which is trained with the samples published in
a specific hour during the day. The tweet time was used in Reference [61] to eliminate the imbal-
anced diurnal effect of user activities, while other temporal factors such as digg time [181], source
time [190], and user activeness variability [210] have been employed to improve model robust-
ness. More simply, some works only explore the items published in daytime to train their models
[29, 38].

   3.1.4  Evolving Trends. Characterizing the evolving trends of information cascades has been
shown to yield informative signals for popularity prediction [8, 74, 116, 224]. Such temporal pat-
terns of time series can be categorized into several types, e.g., smoothly increasing or suddenly
bursting/decaying, dependent on different clustering algorithms. In Figure 6, we show 10 evolving
trends of APS paper citations spanning 20 years by applying an agglomerative hierarchical clus-
tering algorithm [75]. We can see that most of the clusters follow the trend that paper receives
most of its citations in the first few years and then quickly diminishes in the following years.
